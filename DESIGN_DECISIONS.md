# Major Design Decisions

The main choices behind the design, what we compared, the evidence, and how each was decided. AI tools (Claude) were used to analyse options, derive formulas and write code. Every decision below was made by the team. The last column says where the idea or rule came from. D-numbers refer to the team's decision log.

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why (evidence) | How decided |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The 0.5 s horizon is a latency budget.** The total delay from the aim reference to the turret must add up to t_p, so the lead and the lag cancel and the turret points at the drone *now* (D2). | Track θ_pred with no lag; a turret that is slow by itself | The laser is instant. Tracking θ_pred with zero lag would miss by 0.5·v⊥, up to 75 m. With lead and lag matched, the turret points at the current estimate θ̂(t), which uses the newest reading. | Team's own insight (README, 8 Nov), then formalised |
| 2 | **Continuous lag-matched tracking** (D1), not slew-to-prediction-and-freeze | "Option B": slew to one prediction and freeze | B builds in a miss of ½·a⊥·t_p² = 2.5 m at 20 m/s², already above R_eng = 2 m, and needs repeated large slews that ring the ζ = 0.094 plant mode. | Team proposed both options; chosen by analysis |
| 3 | **Hybrid 2-DOF design** (D4): a fast feedback loop plus a prefilter that holds the rest of the lag, with a start value so there is no switch-over | One slow loop with K_v = 2 | It realises the team's "slew first, then track" idea as one LTI design: acquisition at full ±600 V, then lag-matched tracking. No mode switching, as the LTI rule requires. | Team idea, turned into an LTI structure |
| 4 | **Feedback G_c = K_P·N(s)** with K_v = 8 and a notch on the 56 rad/s mode (D18). No integrator (D3). | P only; K_v = 10; a Tustin-discretised notch; integral action | P only at K_v = 8 has GM 2.3 dB and 94 A peaks. The notch gives GM/PM 11.0 dB / 61° at 50 ms, and ≥ 13 dB with ±20 % J or L. K_v = 10 overshoots 16 %. A Tustin notch is unstable at 50 ms. An integrator would remove the deliberate 0.5 s lag. | Classical design (root locus, Bode, margins), verified in MATLAB (`design_controller.m`) |
| 5 | **Kalman filter in Cartesian (x, y)** with converted radar readings | Filtering in (r, θ); fixed-gain α-β-γ | A straight flight is linear in x-y but curved in (r, θ). The PDF's speed and acceleration limits are in x-y. Converted-measurement KF is the standard method [2]. | Team reasoning (README point 7), standard method |
| 6 | **Choose the estimator by the worst case, not the average** | Minimise average error (the earlier plan, D9) | One leaked drone matters more than a good average. The rule was fixed **before** the sweeps: pick by the worst drone type first, then the mean. | Team rule, set before testing |
| 7 | **Singer model** (τ = 1 s, σ_m = 10 m/s²) over constant acceleration (CA) | CA with q from 100 to 20000; Singer τ from 0.25 to 2 s, σ_m from 5 to 20 m/s² | Sweeps on 12 drone types, then a fresh seed. Worst type: Singer 98.1 / 98.2 % vs best CA 97.0 / 98.0 %; the gap is in blackouts. σ_m = 10 is close to Singer's a_max/√3 = 11.5 [1]. CA is kept only to reproduce the comparison. | Test-driven, by the rule in #6 (`estimator_tests/results/`) |
| 8 | **Predictor order n = 1**: θ_pred = θ̂ + t_p·θ̂̇ | n = 2 (+ acceleration term); a Cartesian forecast | Matched-chain test: n = 1 had mean / worst 99.7 / 98 % on target vs 94.9 / 86 % for n = 2. Even the best pure 0.5 s forecast is within 2 m only 60–70 % of the time. With the pairing, the forecast error never reaches the turret, so extra terms only add noise. | Team's Taylor-series reasoning (README point 7), confirmed by tests |
| 9 | **Prefilter with one zero** (D38), matched so F·T·P = 1 up to s² | The first-order prefilter (accepted limitation D33) | The first-order prefilter left a 0.046·r·θ̈ pointing error, about 2.2 m at the envelope corner. With the zero, the residual falls from 0.43 to 0.06 m RMS (Scenario B), and tracking RMS A/B/C goes from 0.36/1.07/1.03 to 0.26/0.75/0.71 m. The cost is 5 % more noise gain. | Team decision after reviewing the integrated code; re-run in full |
| 10 | **Firing rule** (D39): fire when the track is valid, \|d̂\| < 2 m and P_hit ≥ p*, with P_hit = Φ((2 − d̂)/σ) − Φ((−2 − d̂)/σ) | The k-σ bound r̂\|Δθ\| + kσ ≤ R_eng − margin; distance only; an extra turret-rate match | P_hit is the hit probability itself, with one interpretable threshold. The k-σ bound equals a P_hit gate whose threshold drifts between 95.4 and 97.7 %, with two knobs doing one job. Distance alone fires blind in a blackout (σ up to 2.2 m, so only a 64 % hit chance). σ was checked to be honest: 71 / 97 % within 1σ / 2σ. | Team's own rule, derived from first principles (`ESTIMATOR_MATH.md` A11) |
| 11 | **Threshold p* = 0.95**, fitted on the 115-case train set by a rule stated beforehand | 0.5 to 0.999; 0.99 (the earlier value) | 0.95 to 0.995 all give zero missed pulses on train, and 0.95 has the earliest first hits. It was kept instead of switching to 0.99 after seeing results, to avoid a choice made after looking at the numbers. Test: 416/416 hits. Validation: 1438/1438. | Fitted on train by the pre-stated rule (`results/compare_designs.md`) |
| 12 | **Blackout handling:** the estimator coasts and its σ grows, so the gate closes by itself (D5). The prefilter restarts from the corrected estimate when readings return (D27). | Smoothing the reference after a blackout; no restart | Restart ON vs OFF on 88 cases locked before a blackout: worst on-target share 90 % vs 63 %, with no extra misses. | Team proposal, confirmed by a paired case-by-case study |
| 13 | **Robust to the unknown evaluator:** a reading is used only if it is new (not an exact repeat); a new run starts only on a jump back in time of more than 0.5 s; a fixed 50 ms internal grid (D19, D20, D41) | Trust `meas_valid` alone; reset on any step back in time | The instructor's calling style and ODE solver are unknown. A small step back (a rejected solver step) wiped the estimator's track before the D41 fix (shown in MATLAB). Pulse times are identical under clean and solver-style calls. | Team decision; bug found in a cross-module review |
| 14 | **Data split and metrics** (D35, D40): train, test (≈ 2/5 of train, new scenario types) and validation (4 hard categories). Metrics: success, first hit, per-shot hit rate, median miss, consistency (average − worst); estimator bias t-test across runs, error vs sideways acceleration, σ calibration. | One mixed test set; OLS θ̂ = a + b·θ | The PDF grades success, time, accuracy and consistency. The test is one t-test per run, because ticks inside a run are correlated. The OLS a/b fit was dropped because a and b are confounded over one scenario's narrow angle range. | Team specification (`TESTING_VALIDATION_METRICS.md`) |
| 15 | **Test-noise bug fixed** (D32) before any final number | — | The estimate error was twice its own σ. That was traced to correlated "white" noise in the generator (lag-1 autocorrelation 0.69) and replaced by seeded Gaussian noise. | Found by the team's σ-consistency check |
